Pattern method

  • Finished size and round height determine how many joined rounds form the sphere.
  • At each latitude, the local circumference is divided by stitch width to estimate the stitch count, then rounded and optionally smoothed into practical shaping changes.
  • Increase and decrease points are distributed around each round, with optional half-repeat staggering to reduce visible shaping lines.
  • Each stitch footprint is sampled against the equirectangular land mask and assigned a yarn according to the selected coverage thresholds.
  • Orientation and seam controls place the map on the crochet grid without changing the gauge or shaping.
  • The selected stitch layout determines how angular space is assigned within each completed round.

Before you begin

Generated dimensions are estimates rather than guaranteed finished measurements. Tension, stuffing, yarn construction, colour-change technique, joins, and fabric settlement can alter both size and coastline appearance.

Short one-stitch colour runs are preserved. Review them before crocheting and decide whether geographic detail or simpler yarn handling is more important for your project.
